# A Pinch of Salt analysis

A Pinch of Salt — Robert Graves (1916)

Graves makes poetic inspiration a wary bird, teaching the poet when to wait and when to grasp.

Full text from [A Pinch of Salt](https://www.poetry.com/poem-analysis/31093/a-pinch-of-salt). Public domain in the US (Published in Fairies and Fusiliers (1918), before the US public-domain cutoff.).

## Summary

A beautiful dream arrives with painful intensity, but trying to seize it too quickly may damage it. Graves turns that warning into a bird-catching fable.

```
When a dream is born in you
With a sudden clamorous pain,
When you know the dream is true
```

The final stanza advises apparent indifference followed by decisive control.[2]

## Meaning and interpretation

The poem treats inspiration as fragile and independent. Creative discipline means resisting panic first, then shaping the idea once it is ready.[2]

```
Poet, never chase the dream.
Laugh yourself and turn away.
```

The contradiction between detachment and possession describes timing, not passivity.

## Themes

### Inspiration

The dream represents a poem or idea that cannot safely be summoned by force.

### Patience

```
When you seize at the salt-box
Over the hedge you'll see him sail.
```

The bird escapes when desire becomes visibly urgent.

### Creative control

```
But when he nestles in your hand at last,
Close up your fingers tight and hold him fast.
```

The poem distinguishes premature grasping from deliberate final control.

## Form and meter

The poem has three six-line stanzas, with ABABCC, DEDEFF, and GHGHII rhyme patterns. It is predominantly iambic tetrameter.[3]

Short stanzas and closing couplets make the advice sound proverbial.

## Literary devices

### Extended metaphor

```
Dreams are like a bird that mocks,
Flirting the feathers of his tail.
```

The sustained bird image makes inspiration alive, mobile, and resistant to force.

### Imperative voice

Commands addressed to “Poet” turn description into practical artistic counsel.

### Paradox

The speaker says to turn away from the dream, then hold it fast; restraint enables possession.

## Historical context

The poem was published in Fairies and Fusiliers (1918), a collection associated with Graves’s early poetry. Its fable-like subject turns away from direct war experience toward artistic escape.[4]

```
Old birds are neither caught with salt nor chaff:
They watch you from the apple bough and laugh.
```

The rural image gives a modern creative problem the shape of a compact proverb.

## Common questions

### What is the meaning?

Do not force inspiration; wait until the idea approaches naturally, then shape it firmly.

### What does the bird symbolize?

It symbolizes a dream, poem, or creative insight that escapes anxious pursuit.

### What form does it use?

Three six-line stanzas use alternating rhymes and predominantly iambic tetrameter.
