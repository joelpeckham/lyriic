# I Wandered Lonely as a Cloud analysis

I Wandered Lonely as a Cloud — William Wordsworth (1807)

This analysis explains how Wordsworth turns a solitary walk into a meditation on memory and nature.

Full text from [I wandered lonely as a Cloud](https://poets.org/poem/i-wandered-lonely-cloud). Public domain in the US (First published in 1807, before the US public-domain cutoff.).

## Summary

A solitary speaker encounters a vast field of golden daffodils beside a lake; their movement makes nature feel social and alive.[2]

```
I wandered lonely as a Cloud
That floats on high o’er Vales and Hills,
When all at once I saw a crowd,
```

Later, the remembered flowers flash into the speaker’s inward eye and transform solitude into pleasure.[2]

## Meaning and interpretation

Nature is more than a beautiful view: the daffodils become imaginative wealth available after the walk has ended.[2]

```
For oft, when on my couch I lie
In vacant or in pensive mood,
They flash upon that inward eye
```

The poem’s opening loneliness is revised rather than erased; memory creates inward companionship.[3]

## Themes

### Nature and imagination

The mind turns flowers into a crowd, host, stars, and dancers.

### Memory

The final stanza makes recollection the source of lasting joy.[2]

### Solitude and companionship

The speaker begins alone but ends with nature as remembered company.

## Form and meter

Four six-line stanzas use ABABCC rhyme; the closing couplets give each scene a musical resolution.[3]

The lines are chiefly iambic tetrameter, whose steady movement supports the poem’s shift from wandering to dancing.[3]

## Literary devices

### Simile

```
I wandered lonely as a Cloud
```

The speaker appears detached and drifting before the flowers create companionship.

### Personification

```
Fluttering and dancing in the breeze.
```

Human movement makes natural motion into emotional communication.

### Hyperbole

```
Ten thousand saw I at a glance,
Tossing their heads in sprightly dance.
```

The vast number enlarges the scene into a memorable vision.

## Historical context

Wordsworth composed the poem in 1804 after the 1802 Ullswater walk with Dorothy and first published it in 1807; the familiar four-stanza version was revised in 1815.[2]

Wordsworth Grasmere links the later stanza to the poet’s idea of emotion “recollected in tranquillity.”[2]

## Common questions

### What is the poem’s meaning?

An encounter with nature becomes lasting joy when memory returns it to the inward eye.

### What do the daffodils symbolize?

Natural vitality, companionship, and the imaginative wealth of remembered experience.

### What form does it use?

Four six-line stanzas with ABABCC rhyme and chiefly iambic tetrameter.
