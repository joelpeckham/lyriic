# In Praise of Solid People analysis

In Praise of Solid People — C. S. Lewis (1919)

This In Praise of Solid People analysis explains the poem’s meaning, themes, form, and literary devices.

Full text from [In Praise of Solid People](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2003/2003-h/2003-h.htm). Public domain in the US (First published in 1919 or earlier, placing this text in the public domain in the United States.).

## Summary

The speaker envies ordinary people whose domestic routines and settled affections seem peaceful. Lonely nights, phantom faces, and impossible visions reveal the cost of his restless longing.

```
Thank God that there are solid folk
Who water flowers and roll the lawn,
And sit an sew and talk and smoke,
```

The poem’s structure makes its central tension memorable.[1]

## Meaning and interpretation

The poem reverses the speaker’s earlier contempt for ordinary life. “Weariness and strife” teach him that practical affection and contentment may be wisdom rather than dullness.[1]

```
And still no neared to the Light,
And still no further from myself,
Alone and lost in clinging night—
```

Its images turn an abstract concern into a scene readers can hear and see.[2]

## Themes

### Contentment versus desire

The poem returns to contentment versus desire through recurring images and shifts in voice.[1]

### Loneliness and spiritual uncertainty

```
And still no neared to the Light,
And still no further from myself,
Alone and lost in clinging night—
```

This theme is developed through the poem’s contrast between image and argument.[2]

## Form and meter

Thirteen quatrains use mostly alternating rhyme and a generally iambic-tetrameter pulse. The regular units resemble the stability the speaker admires, even as syntax and imagery become troubled.[2]

```
Thank God that there are solid folk
Who water flowers and roll the lawn,
And sit an sew and talk and smoke,
```
