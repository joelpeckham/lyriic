## Summary

The speaker addresses an urn whose scenes preserve music, desire, ritual, and beauty beyond human lifetimes. The figures escape decay but remain frozen in incomplete actions.[1]

```
Heard melodies are sweet, but those unheard
Are sweeter; therefore, ye soft pipes, play on;
```

## Meaning and interpretation

Keats presents art as both consolation and limitation. The final statement invites interpretation rather than closing every question about beauty and truth.[2]

```
When old age shall this generation waste,
Thou shalt remain, in midst of other woe
Than ours, a friend to man,
```
