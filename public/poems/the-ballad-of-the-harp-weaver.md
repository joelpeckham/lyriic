# The Ballad of the Harp-Weaver analysis

The Ballad of the Harp-Weaver — Edna St. Vincent Millay (1922)

This analysis explains how a mother’s magical gift exposes the costs of poverty, care, and gendered labor.

Full text from [The Ballad of the Harp-Weaver](https://poets.org/poem/ballad-harp-weaver). Public domain in the US (First published in 1922, before the US public-domain cutoff.).

## Summary

A poor mother has no cloth, food, or fuel for her son. On Christmas Eve she uses her harp to weave him warm clothing.

```
“Son,” said my mother,
When I was knee-high,
“You’ve need of clothes to cover you,
```

The child wakes to find the clothes beside his mother, whose hands are frozen in the harp-strings. The miracle is inseparable from its cost.[3]

## Meaning and interpretation

Maternal love is inventive and immense, but the poem refuses to make sacrifice uncomplicated. The clothing saves the child while revealing the conditions that consume the person providing care.

```
She sang as she worked,
And the harp-strings spoke;
Her voice never faltered,
```

The harp changes from an object nobody will buy into an instrument of creation, making neglected artistic power visible.[3]

## Themes

### Maternal love and sacrifice

The mother turns her last strength into protection, and the miraculous clothing makes the fatal cost of that care visible.

### Poverty and neglect

```
Men say the winter
Was bad that year;
Fuel was scarce,
```

The repeated lack of clothing, food, and fuel makes private love carry a burden that society has failed to share.

### Women’s labor and art

The mother is musician and weaver, but her creative identity is recognized only when it serves the child.[3]

## Form and meter

The poem is a narrative ballad in thirty mostly quatrain stanzas, with a largely ABCB rhyme pattern and flexible accentual rhythm.

Repetition and sound effects—“She wove,” “Looking nineteen,” and “weav-weav-weaving”—make the story feel oral and song-like.[3]

## Literary devices

### Personification

```
A wind with a wolf’s head
Howled about our door
```

The wolf-headed wind makes winter an active predator and intensifies the family’s isolation.

### Symbolism

```
And a harp with a woman’s head
Nobody will buy
```

The harp represents the mother’s neglected creative identity before becoming the source of survival.

### Irony

```
Were the clothes of a king’s son,
Just my size.
```

Regal clothing appears in a house without food or fuel, making triumph and loss occupy the same image.

## Historical context

The poem was first published in 1922 and later gave its name to Millay’s Pulitzer Prize-winning collection. The Academy of American Poets identifies it as public domain.[1]

Its traditional ballad frame places a poor mother and child inside a fairy-tale structure, then makes the miracle inseparable from class deprivation and gendered labor.

## Common questions

### What is the meaning?

The poem portrays maternal love as creative and powerful while showing that survival depends on a devastating sacrifice made under poverty.

### Why does the mother die?

She spends the cold night weaving clothes for her son, and her hands are frozen in the harp-strings by morning.

### What does the harp symbolize?

It represents neglected artistic identity and the creative power that transforms music into protection.
