# The Rape of the Lock analysis

The Rape of the Lock — Alexander Pope (1714)

This analysis of Alexander Pope’s The Rape of the Lock explains how its heroic couplets and miniature epic machinery turn a stolen lock of hair into a sharp, comic study of vanity, status, and conflict.

## The poem

```
Close by those meads, for ever crown'd with flow'rs,
Where Thames with pride surveys his rising tow'rs,
There stands a structure of majestic frame,
Which from the neighb'ring Hampton takes its name.
Here Britain's statesmen oft the fall foredoom
Of foreign Tyrants and of Nymphs at home;
Here thou, great Anna! whom three realms obey.
Dost sometimes counsel take — and sometimes Tea.

Hither the heroes and the nymphs resort,
To taste awhile the pleasures of a Court;
In various talk th' instructive hours they past,
Who gave the ball, or paid the visit last;
One speaks the glory of the British Queen,
And one describes a charming Indian screen;
A third interprets motions, looks, and eyes;
At ev'ry word a reputation dies.
Snuff, or the fan, supply each pause of chat,
With singing, laughing, ogling, and all that.

Mean while, declining from the noon of day,
The sun obliquely shoots his burning ray;
The hungry Judges soon the sentence sign,
And wretches hang that jury-men may dine;
The merchant from th' Exchange returns in peace,
And the long labours of the Toilet cease.

Belinda now, whom thirst of fame invites,
Burns to encounter two advent'rous Knights,
At Ombre singly to decide their doom;
And swells her breast with conquests yet to come.
Straight the three bands prepare in arms to join,
Each band the number of the sacred nine.
Soon as she spreads her hand, th' aërial guard
Descend, and sit on each important card:
First Ariel perch'd upon a Matadore,
Then each, according to the rank they bore;
For Sylphs, yet mindful of their ancient race,
Are, as when women, wondrous fond of place.

Behold, four Kings in majesty rever'd,
With hoary whiskers and a forky beard;
And four fair Queens whose hands sustain a flow'r,
Th' expressive emblem of their softer pow'r;
Four Knaves in garbs succinct, a trusty band,
Caps on their heads and halberts in their hand;
And particolour'd troops, a shining train,
Draw forth to combat on the velvet plain.

The skilful Nymph reviews her force with care:
Let Spades be trumps! she said, and trumps they were.
Now move to war her sable Matadores,
In show like leaders of the swarthy Moors.
Spadillio first, unconquerable Lord!
Led off two captive trumps, and swept the board.
As many more Manillio forc'd to yield,
And march'd a victor from the verdant field.

Just then, Clarissa drew with tempting grace
A two-edg'd weapon from her shining case:
So Ladies in Romance assist their Knight,
Present the spear, and arm him for the fight.
He takes the gift with rev'rence, and extends
The little engine on his fingers' ends;
This just behind Belinda's neck he spread,
As o'er the fragrant steams she bends her head.
Swift to the Lock a thousand Sprites repair,
A thousand wings, by turns, blow back the hair;
And thrice they twitch'd the diamond in her ear;
Thrice she look'd back, and thrice the foe drew near.

Just in that instant, anxious Ariel sought
The close recesses of the Virgin's thought;
As on the nosegay in her breast reclin'd,
He watch'd th' Ideas rising in her mind,
Sudden he view'd, in spite of all her art,
An earthly Lover lurking at her heart.
Amaz'd, confus'd, he found his pow'r expir'd,
Resign'd to fate, and with a sigh retir'd.
The Peer now spreads the glitt'ring Forfex wide,
T' inclose the Lock; now joins it, to divide.
Ev'n then, before the fatal engine clos'd,
A wretched Sylph too fondly interpos'd;
Fate urg'd the shears, and cut the Sylph in twain,
(But airy substance soon unites again)
The meeting points the sacred hair dissever
From the fair head, for ever, and for ever!
```

This excerpt presents the opening of Canto III, the mock-epic card battle, and the famous scissors scene. Read the complete five-canto poem at the linked Project Gutenberg edition.

## Summary

Pope turns a quarrel over the cutting of Belinda’s hair into a five-canto epic. Belinda visits Hampton Court, plays ombre, and becomes the target of the Baron, who wants a lock of her hair; sylphs attempt to protect her, but the Baron cuts it with Clarissa’s scissors.

The poem’s deliberately grand scale makes the social incident look both ridiculous and revealing. The card game becomes warfare, a dressing table becomes an altar, and the lock becomes a prize whose loss exposes the characters’ ideas about beauty, honor, and possession.

## Meaning and interpretation

The poem’s central meaning comes from its mismatch between subject and style. Pope uses the language, supernatural machinery, and battle conventions of epic poetry for a fashionable dispute, making the characters’ exaggerated responses comic while showing how seriously status and reputation govern their world.

The satire is not simply a joke at Belinda’s expense. The Baron’s desire is described through the language of conquest, and the poem gives both men and women vanity, aggression, and social performance. Clarissa’s later appeal to good sense offers an alternative to the conflict, but the crowd ignores her, suggesting that the society enjoys its rituals of grievance too much to accept moderation.

## Themes

### Triviality and social importance

A lock of hair is materially small, yet the poem treats its theft as a crisis of honor. The contrast satirizes a culture that gives ceremonial weight to appearance and gossip.

### Gender, desire, and possession

The Baron frames the lock as a prize to be won, while Belinda’s beauty is treated as public spectacle. Epic language makes the unequal power and sexual undertones of this flirtation visible beneath the comedy.

### Vanity and performance

Court conversation, cosmetics, cards, fans, and fashionable objects form a world in which identity is staged. The characters defend not only feelings but also the public image attached to those performances.

### Satire with sympathy

Pope mocks the fashionable world’s shallow priorities, but he also enjoys its wit, ornament, and sociability. The poem’s tone is therefore amused and corrective rather than purely hostile.

## Form and meter

The poem is a mock epic in five cantos, written chiefly in heroic couplets: rhymed pairs of iambic pentameter lines. The couplets give each thought a compact, balanced finish while allowing Pope to place a lofty idea beside a mundane one.

Epic conventions include an invocation, supernatural beings, formal speeches, battle scenes, a descent to an underworld-like cave, and a final transformation into a constellation. Pope miniaturizes these conventions around cards, cosmetics, scissors, and social reputation.

The excerpt’s Canto III uses the syntax and diction of military narrative to describe ombre. Its regular couplets make the card game feel orderly even as the inflated comparison turns ordinary competition into mock warfare.

## Literary devices

### Mock-epic parody

> Let Spades be trumps! she said, and trumps they were. / Now move to war her sable Matadores

The card game is narrated as a military campaign. Pope borrows epic scale and diction to expose the gap between the form of the action and its modest stakes.

### Bathos

> Here Britain's statesmen oft the fall foredoom / Of foreign Tyrants and of Nymphs at home;

A grand political phrase collapses into fashionable domestic gossip. The sudden descent from national affairs to nymphs is a characteristic comic deflation.

### Personification

> The Peer now spreads the glitt'ring Forfex wide, / T' inclose the Lock; now joins it, to divide.

The scissors receive an elevated name and function like a weapon in a heroic encounter, helping an object become an agent of mock-epic fate.

### Allusion

> Fate urg'd the shears, and cut the Sylph in twain

References to epic fate and heroic combat place the hair-cutting inside a literary tradition far larger than the event itself. The allusions create both admiration for epic form and comic disproportion.

## Historical context

Pope first published a shorter, two-canto version in 1712 after a quarrel between Arabella Fermor and Lord Robert Petre. He expanded the poem to five cantos in 1714, adding the sylphs and other supernatural machinery.

The poem is set in the fashionable world of early eighteenth-century England, including Hampton Court, court visits, ombre, cosmetics, coffee, and elaborate dress. These details give the satire a specific social setting rather than an abstract target.

Pope’s dedication explains that the poem was meant to let its subjects laugh at “their sex's little unguarded follies” and at themselves. That framing helps explain the poem’s combination of social criticism, personal tact, and playful exaggeration.

## Common questions

### What is the main meaning of The Rape of the Lock?

Pope uses an epic style to make a fashionable quarrel over a lock of hair look enormous. The comic mismatch criticizes vanity, status anxiety, and the ways desire and honor become contests for possession.

### Why is The Rape of the Lock called a mock epic?

It imitates epic conventions—invocation, supernatural machinery, heroic battles, speeches, and elevated language—but applies them to cards, cosmetics, court gossip, and scissors. The imitation both honors epic poetry and makes fashionable pretensions look absurd.

### What is the form and meter of the poem?

The poem is organized into five cantos and is chiefly written in heroic couplets: rhymed pairs of iambic pentameter lines. The balanced couplets sharpen Pope’s wit and his contrasts between grand and trivial subjects.

### What happens in the scissors scene?

Clarissa gives the Baron a pair of scissors, which Pope describes as a weapon. Ariel discovers an earthly lover in Belinda’s heart and loses his protective power; the Baron then cuts off the lock despite a sylph’s attempt to intervene.

### What do the sylphs represent?

The sylphs are comic supernatural guardians of fashionable women and their reputations. They make social vanity look like an entire spiritual system, while also parodying the gods and spirits of classical epic.
