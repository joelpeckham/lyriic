# The Tyger analysis

The Tyger — William Blake (1794)

Blake’s repeated questions and forge imagery make creation both beautiful and frightening, without resolving the mystery.

Full text from [The Tyger](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/43687/the-tyger). Public domain in the US (Published in Songs of Experience in 1794 and public domain in the United States.).

## Summary

The speaker addresses a tiger whose beauty and violence provoke questions about the identity and courage of its maker.[1]

```
Tyger Tyger, burning bright,
In the forests of the night;
What immortal hand or eye,
```

The closing stanza repeats the opening but changes “Could” to “Dare,” intensifying the question from ability to audacity.[4]

## Meaning and interpretation

“Fearful symmetry” joins order and terror. Blake does not reduce the Tyger to simple evil; unanswered questions keep its moral meaning open.[2]

```
What the hammer? what the chain,
In what furnace was thy brain?
What the anvil? what dread grasp.
```

The question about the Lamb places innocence and experience in one creation, making contradiction central to the poem.[4]

## Themes

### Creation and mystery

The speaker can imagine a maker but cannot explain or identify that maker; uncertainty is the poem’s central condition.[4]

### Beauty and terror

```
Could frame thy fearful symmetry?
```

The phrase holds attraction and danger together in a single image.

### Innocence and experience

“Did he who made the Lamb make thee?” connects this poem to Blake’s companion poem and its different moral atmosphere.[4]

## Form and meter

The poem has six quatrains and is commonly read as catalectic trochaic tetrameter. The falling beat suits the hammering forge imagery.[1]

Repeated opening and closing stanzas create a circular structure, while the final “Dare” marks its key variation.

## Literary devices

### Rhetorical questions

```
What immortal hand or eye,
Could frame thy fearful symmetry?
```

The accumulation of questions makes uncertainty a formal effect.[4]

### Forge imagery

```
What the hammer? what the chain,
In what furnace was thy brain?
```

Tools recast creation as physical, forceful labor.[2]

### Repetition and variation

```
Could frame thy fearful symmetry?
... Dare frame thy fearful symmetry?
```

The altered verb changes the poem’s final pressure from capacity to moral nerve.

## Historical context

Blake published the poem in Songs of Experience (1794), paired conceptually with Songs of Innocence and its poem “The Lamb.”[4]

Blake was both poet and engraver; the image of a maker shaping a creature through fire resonates with his material art practice.[4]

## Common questions

### What is the main meaning of The Tyger?

It asks how one creator could make something both beautiful and terrifying, and leaves the question unresolved.

### What does the Tyger symbolize?

It can suggest fierce energy, danger, sublime power, or an unknowable aspect of creation.

### What meter does it use?

It is commonly read as catalectic trochaic tetrameter.
